# Understanding materials, printing and finishes

This glossary helps a buyer describe a packaging request. It is educational guidance, not a list of approved options for a particular product. The Pack Legacy materials library discusses the terms below, but appearance in that library or a product search does not establish availability, compatibility, certification, strength or price for an order. Confirm the selected combination during the quote and artwork review.

## Structure and material

**Folding paperboard** is board cut and creased into a carton. **Corrugated board** includes a fluted layer bonded to liner sheets; the flute and board construction are part of the specification. **Rigid board or chipboard** describes the substantial board used in rigid packaging, commonly combined with a separate covering paper. These categories help describe a request, but they do not determine a safe load or a suitable product on their own.

**Kraft** can describe a paper or board used in different packaging constructions. A request for “kraft” should also identify the structure, desired surface and intended use. Do not assume that the word guarantees a recycled content, a particular thickness or a finished package's environmental performance.

**GSM** means grams per square metre, a measure of mass per area. **PT** refers to thickness in points. **Flute** describes the corrugated layer. These terms measure different properties, so a GSM value cannot be converted into a confirmed thickness or strength without the actual material specification. If uncertain, record the desired feel and protection needs instead of choosing a number.

## Printing and artwork

Printing places the design on the material. Digital and offset are printing methods; CMYK and named spot colours describe colour requirements. Record logos, text, colours, coverage and the surfaces to be printed as requests. The selected material and printing process affect the result, so a screen preview is not a guaranteed colour match.

A **dieline** identifies the layout used to position artwork relative to the package's cuts and folds. Artwork placement, bleed and safe areas need review against the correct structural design. Downloading a template or marking artwork ready does not establish that the file is approved for manufacture.

## Surface appearance and texture

**Matte** and **gloss** describe surface appearance. A coating and a lamination are different finishing processes; record the desired effect if the buyer has not chosen the process. **Soft touch** describes a tactile finish that may be produced through different coatings or laminations.

**Spot UV** applies a UV-cured coating to selected areas. **Foil stamping** adds a foil effect to a design; the required process and foil must be confirmed. **Embossing** raises an area of the surface, while **debossing** depresses it. **Window patching** combines an opening with a covering film. A request for any of these effects still needs checks for the selected stock, artwork, structure and production method.

## Inserts and performance requirements

An insert holds or separates contents inside the package. Cardboard and EVA foam are examples discussed in the materials library. The insert's shape, thickness, clearances and contact with the product need review together with the box dimensions. Finding both a box and an insert in the catalogue does not prove they form a compatible set.

Describe practical needs such as fragility, product weight, transport, food contact or environmental claims. Treat these as requirements for the specialist to confirm with suitable specifications or evidence; do not infer certifications or performance from a material name. Samples can help compare appearance and feel, but their scope and relation to the final production specification should be agreed.
